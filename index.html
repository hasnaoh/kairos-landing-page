Pricing Intelligence for Luxury Resale

THE MOMENT
THAT MATTERS

In ancient Greek, kairos (καιρός) names not the passage of time but the quality of a specific moment — the instant when conditions are exactly right and action is correct. It was the fourth pillar of rhetoric, beside logos, ethos, and pathos. Timing, the Greeks argued, is not a footnote. It is an argument.

Kairos applies that logic to luxury resale pricing. Not just what an item is worth — but whether now is the right moment to list it.

The Problem

THE MARKDOWN DEATH SPIRAL

Luxury resale is a market where an item's value is not fixed — it is a function of condition, provenance, timing, and the signals that surround it. When an item is listed at the wrong price, the market does not correct quietly. It cascades.

An overpriced listing sits. The platform's algorithm reduces its visibility. A markdown is issued. That markdown signals distress to buyers trained to wait for reductions. The next markdown arrives sooner. By the time the item clears, it has sold for less than a correct initial price would have generated — and has conditioned buyers to expect discounts on everything that follows.

This is the markdown death spiral. It is not a rounding error. At the scale of a major resale platform, it is a structural revenue problem — and it starts with a mispriced listing.

The Kairos Insight

COMPARABLE SALES TELL YOU WHAT AN ITEM SOLD FOR.
THEY DO NOT TELL YOU WHETHER THIS IS THE RIGHT WEEK TO LIST IT.

Most pricing tools in luxury resale are backward-looking. They compare an incoming item to what similar items cleared for, then surface a number. That is a necessary input. It is not a sufficient one.

Consumer search behavior for specific brands, categories, and aesthetics fluctuates. A piece listed during a period of declining search interest will clear at a lower price — or will not clear at all — compared to the same piece listed three weeks earlier when interest was building. The comp-based model misses this entirely.

Kairos adds a timing signal derived from real-time search trend data, lagged two weeks to match the consignment-to-listing lifecycle. The output is not just a price. It is a recommendation about when to act on that price.

Rising

Hold or stretch price. Demand is building. Early markdown is measurable value destruction.

Flat

Price to model. Comp-based estimate is accurate. Standard listing strategy applies.

Declining

Consider delay or immediate discount. Market is cooling. Full price increases markdown risk.

How It Works

THREE STAGES. ONE OUTPUT.

01 — Input

Describe the Item

Submit a natural language description. Brand, condition, creative context, accessories. No rigid structured fields — the system reads what a merchandiser would actually write.

02 — Analyze

Multi-Layer Intelligence

A feature extraction layer structures the description. A regression model trained on historical resale transactions produces a price estimate with a quantile confidence range. A timing layer reads current market demand.

03 — Output

Price, Range, Signal, Rationale

Recommended listing price. Confidence band. Trend signal — rising, flat, or declining. And the reasoning behind each component, readable without a data background.
